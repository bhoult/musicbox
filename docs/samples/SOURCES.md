## Processing

- Decoded and resampled to 48 kHz with ffmpeg (soxr where available), 16-bit with triangular dither; channels as recorded (stereo, but for the harmonica, recorded in mono).
- Leading silence trimmed: the onset is the first sample within 50 dB of the file's peak, and the file starts 1 ms before it (that 1 ms faded in).
- Tail cut at the end of the last 10 ms that reaches -60 dB relative to the peak, with a 50 ms raised-cosine fade (a quarter of the sample for samples under 200 ms). To fit the disk budget, samples of the articulations bow 5.5 s, clash 4 s, hit 4 s, open 2.5 s, pluck 4.5 s, quinto 2 s, ring 10 s, roll 3 s, small_hit 2.5 s, soft 4.5 s, sus 5.5 s, tumba 2.5 s, vib 5.5 s are capped at that many seconds after the onset, the last 0.5 s faded out.
- Roots: the note in each file name, corrected per folder (most VSCO-2-CE and VCSL folders name notes one octave low, the C3 = middle C convention) and verified by pitch estimation of every sample; see `ROOT_FIX_FOLDER` in `tools/samples_manifest.py`. Unpitched percussion uses root 60.
- Retuned (resampled to its root, the length changing with it): 26 files of the Tanzanian mbira, tuned to its own scale (-58 to +63 cents); see `TUNE_FILE` in `tools/samples_manifest.py`.
- Retuned the same way: the gamelan's gong (VCSL's Gong 1, 3 files, +1039 cents), so that its root 60 is its pitch (the same files untuned are STARCONFLICT's and WESTERN's gong).
- Velocity layers split 0-127 evenly, softest first. `gain_db` is the instrument/articulation's gain, putting the loudest layer's median RMS over the first 1 s after the onset at -20 dBFS, plus a trim per root and layer that brings each to the loudest layer's median K-weighted (ITU-R BS.1770) level, over 1 s after the onset for the sustained articulations (sus, vib, bow) and 0.3 s for the rest: the recordings are uneven from note to note, and the player scales a note by its velocity already. A trim raises a file at most 15 dB and lowers it at most 24 dB; `sources.tsv` lists each file's level and trim.
